Executive verdict

Priority: turn Neckermann Strom from a compact sales website into the clearest German source for dynamic electricity tariffs, Smart Meter requirements, price mechanics, switching, and consumer protections.

The live site has indexable service pages, a tariff calculator, a useful FAQ, an Hamburg location page, direct contact details, and complete corporate identification. Search engines already surface core tariff and service pages. The weakness is depth and connected authority. Several pages repeat broad claims while offering limited original data, named expertise, citations, comparison tables, or structured entity markup.

AI systems favor content they can retrieve, understand, verify, and quote. Google states its generative search features rely on the same core search systems and recommends strong technical SEO plus unique, expert-led, people-first content. Neckermann Strom needs no separate “AI trick.” It needs better evidence, entities, answers, and architecture.

AI visibility assessment

What already helps

  • Clear descriptions of dynamic tariffs, renewable electricity, switching, billing, and Smart Meter use.
  • Question-and-answer content on one crawlable FAQ page.
  • Consistent company contact information and a separate customer portal.
  • A location page connecting Neckermann Strom with Hamburg search intent.

What holds citations back

  • No visible author or expert reviewer on key educational pages.
  • Few cited primary sources such as Bundesnetzagentur, BDEW, SMARD, Verbraucherzentrale, or applicable legislation.
  • Few unique facts, calculations, charts, dated tariff examples, downloadable resources, or original analysis.
  • Limited structured data for Organization, WebSite, BreadcrumbList, Service, Article, and appropriate FAQ content.
  • Claims such as “100% Ökostrom,” savings potential, and transparent pricing need linked proof and defined methodology.
  • Content does not fully answer high-intent questions in concise blocks AI systems can quote with confidence.

Google’s current guidance treats AI visibility as an extension of strong SEO. It specifically prioritizes crawlable technical foundations and unique, reliable, people-first content. Google also states it does not use llms.txt for Search or its generative features.

90-day improvement plan

Days 1–15. Measurement and technical control

  1. Verify Google Search Console, GA4, Google Ads, consent mode, call tracking, and calculator funnel events.
  2. Export every indexable URL. Map status code, canonical, title, meta description, H1, word count, internal links, index status, and organic performance.
  3. Validate robots.txt, XML sitemaps, canonicals, redirects, pagination, image indexing, and duplicate `/home/` versus root behavior.
  4. Run mobile and desktop Core Web Vitals tests on the homepage, tariff pages, Hamburg page, FAQ, contact form, and calculator result flow.
  5. Validate structured data and deploy one clean Organization entity graph across the site.

Days 16–45. Authority and answer architecture

  1. Build a definitive “How dynamic electricity prices work” guide with formula, example bill, risks, ideal customer profiles, glossary, and cited primary sources.
  2. Create expert-reviewed guides for Smart Meters, heat-pump electricity, night-storage heating, switching, billing, cancellation, and price composition.
  3. Add visible author, expert reviewer, publish date, update date, sources, and editorial policy.
  4. Expand FAQs around real customer decisions. Link each short answer to a detailed guide.
  5. Create the current-company entity page. Explain ownership, leadership, licensing or registrations, energy sourcing, service territory, and brand history.

Days 46–90. Search expansion

  1. Create differentiated regional pages only where pricing, network operators, service, or useful local facts support unique content.
  2. Publish monthly spot-market analysis using SMARD or EPEX-linked data and a repeatable methodology.
  3. Add comparison pages for dynamic versus fixed tariffs, with balanced suitability and risk analysis.
  4. Strengthen internal linking from educational pages into relevant calculator states and service pages.
  5. Earn authentic citations through data studies, partnerships, energy publications, local business profiles, and expert commentary.

Ten highest-impact actions

  1. Resolve the root versus `/home/` duplication and confirm one canonical homepage.
  2. Install complete Organization and WebSite entity markup with consistent legal identifiers.
  3. Add expert authorship, review credentials, dates, and cited primary sources.
  4. Publish a definitive dynamic-tariff guide with a worked German household example.
  5. Explain the current Neckermann Strom GmbH and separate it clearly from historic Neckermann entities.
  6. Measure the complete calculator-to-contract funnel in GA4 and Google Ads.
  7. Validate Core Web Vitals on real mobile templates and reduce image, font, script, and builder overhead.
  8. Expand FAQs into deeper topic clusters for Smart Meter, heat pump, night storage, price risk, switching, and billing.
  9. Create original monthly electricity-price analysis with charts, methodology, and source links.
  10. Build a controlled internal-link system connecting every guide, FAQ, tariff, regional page, and conversion route.
