PLAN-B Contest Compliance Workspace

The decisions, disclosures, and website changes needed to prepare a German consumer giveaway linked to an electricity offer.

25 questions for the client

These answers are required before final rules, advertisements, forms, and privacy language can be approved.

  1. What is the full legal name and address of the company running the giveaway?
  2. Is that same company the electricity supplier?
  3. If not, what is the electricity supplier’s full legal name?
  4. Is this PLAN-B’s own giveaway, or is an outside lottery company involved?
  5. Who funds and legally guarantees the prizes?
  6. Is the main prize exactly €1,000,000 in cash?
  7. Is it paid as one lump sum?
  8. What other prizes or instant prizes are included?
  9. How many prizes are available?
  10. What is the total documented value of all prizes?
  11. What are the giveaway’s start and end dates?
  12. On what date will the winner be selected?
  13. Is participation limited to German residents aged 18 or older?
  14. Which PLAN-B electricity tariffs qualify?
  15. Does every qualifying customer receive one entry?
  16. When does the entry become valid: after application, contract acceptance, expiration of the 14-day withdrawal period, or commencement of electricity service?
  17. What happens to the entry if the customer withdraws or cancels the contract?
  18. Does any part of the electricity price pay for the giveaway entry?
  19. Will people have a free way to enter without signing an electricity contract?
  20. If yes, how will free entry work?
  21. How will the winner be selected and documented?
  22. How will PLAN-B contact the winner, and how long will the winner have to respond?
  23. What personal information will be collected from entrants?
  24. Will entrants receive advertising emails, calls, or text messages?
  25. Does PLAN-B intend to publish the winner’s name, city, photograph, or video?

Executive answer

Recommended structure: operate this as a transparent promotional sweepstakes with free participation, or a genuinely equivalent free entry route. Keep any electricity contract and marketing consent legally separate from entry wherever possible.

A purchase-linked promotion is not automatically unlawful in Germany. The design becomes much riskier if participants effectively pay a stake for a random chance to win, if the promotion amounts to unlicensed lottery brokerage, or if the commercial terms are hidden or misleading. German counsel should approve the final mechanics before launch.

Classification to confirm

  • Promotional sweepstakes: no separately priced stake, clear rules, and a genuine promotional purpose.
  • Paid lottery or gambling: payment is required for a chance-based prize. This can trigger the German Interstate Treaty on Gambling and licensing rules.
  • Lottery mediation: an external operator or broker is involved. Roles, permissions, and advertising restrictions must be checked.

Advertising and claims

  • Energy savings: “protect yourself from high electricity costs” and similar claims need reliable evidence, clear comparison assumptions, and any material limitations.
  • Prize pool: “prizes worth millions” must state the exact aggregate value, number and type of prizes, and valuation basis.
  • Grand prize: “1 Mio.” must identify the currency, whether it is cash, the exact amount, and whether it is a lump sum.
  • Automatic entry: explain exactly which tariff qualifies and when an entry becomes valid, including the effect of withdrawal or cancellation.
  • Responsible parties: name the promoter, electricity supplier, prize sponsor, and any external lottery operator.
  • Environmental claims: “Net Zero” and green-energy claims require a specific, documented basis. Avoid broad claims that cannot be substantiated.

Participation terms checklist

  • Promoter’s legal name, address, and contact details.
  • Entry period, time zone, closing time, and rules version.
  • Eligible territory, minimum age, customer status, and excluded persons.
  • Free entry method and any limits per person or household.
  • Qualifying tariffs and the exact point at which entry becomes effective.
  • Consequences of rejected applications, withdrawal, revocation, cancellation, and supply failure.
  • Number, description, value, availability, transferability, and delivery of every prize.
  • Random-selection method, independent oversight where appropriate, draw date, notification, response deadline, reserves, and verification.
  • Disqualification rules for fraud or abuse that are objective and proportionate.
  • Taxes, costs, substitutions, force majeure, liability, governing law, consumer rights, and complaints contact.
  • Data use, retention, recipients, winner publicity, and links to the privacy notice.
  • No misleading “legal recourse excluded” clause that attempts to remove mandatory consumer rights.

Electricity contract and checkout

Before the consumer places an order, present the essential contract information clearly and prominently. This includes supplier identity, total price and price components, billing and consumption assumptions, contract duration, renewal, cancellation, start of supply, complaint handling, and withdrawal rights. The contest must not obscure the tariff economics.

Order button: for a paid online energy contract, the final button should use the statutory wording “Zahlungspflichtig bestellen” or an equally unambiguous formulation.

Cancellation flow

Where the German cancellation-button rules apply, provide a continuously available first-stage button labelled “Verträge hier kündigen”, followed by a confirmation page with a final button labelled “Jetzt kündigen”. Supply immediate electronic confirmation and preserve the consumer’s submitted cancellation data.

Privacy, cookies, and marketing

  • Use only the data necessary to administer entry, verify eligibility, select and contact winners, prevent fraud, and fulfil prizes.
  • Map a lawful basis for each purpose. Do not treat contest entry as blanket consent to advertising.
  • Use separate, unticked choices for contest participation, email marketing, telephone marketing, SMS marketing, and winner publicity where those choices rely on consent.
  • Record consent text, timestamp, source, version, and withdrawal. Make withdrawal as easy as consent.
  • Set retention periods for entrants, winners, reserves, fraud records, and consent evidence.
  • Put processor agreements and international transfer safeguards in place before sharing data with vendors.
  • Do not load analytics or advertising cookies until valid consent is obtained, unless a tag is strictly necessary.

Evidence and operational records

  • Signed prize-funding or guarantee documentation.
  • Prize inventory, valuation evidence, and fulfilment contracts.
  • Approved rules, advertisements, landing pages, and consent language with version history.
  • Substantiation file for savings, price comparisons, “Net Zero,” and prize-value claims.
  • Auditable entry ledger, deduplication rules, random draw method, draw log, witnesses, and reserve-winner process.
  • Consent logs, cookie records, data-processing agreements, deletion schedule, and incident process.
  • Customer-service scripts for entry status, withdrawal, cancellation, winner contact, and complaints.
  • Monitoring and takedown process for affiliates, social ads, and expired campaigns.

Ten launch blockers

  1. Promoter, supplier, sponsor, or operator identity is unresolved.
  2. Entry requires a paid stake or the lottery classification is uncertain.
  3. No approved free-entry route where one is needed to reduce gambling and unfairness risk.
  4. Prize funding or the €1,000,000 cash prize is not contractually guaranteed.
  5. Entry timing and the effect of withdrawal or cancellation are unclear.
  6. Advertising claims lack evidence or omit material conditions.
  7. Participation terms and privacy notice are incomplete or inconsistent.
  8. Checkout information, order-button wording, or cancellation flow is non-compliant.
  9. Marketing consent is bundled, preselected, or required for entry without a valid basis.
  10. The draw, winner verification, data retention, and fulfilment process has not been tested and documented.
